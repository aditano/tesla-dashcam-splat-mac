# Tesla dashcam splat tool: AI methods research

Date: 2026-11-22 (ET). Repo: aditano/tesla-dashcam-splat (main at a6bab62, Round 3). Live: https://aditano.github.io/tesla-dashcam-splat/
Scope: research only, no code changes.

Current pipeline (for reference): SEI telemetry (GPS, heading, speed) gives rig poses -> Depth Anything V2 Small (`onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small`, transformers.js, WebGPU or WASM) per frame -> metric scale from camera height plus odometry -> back-project to gaussians -> fuse -> short consensus refine -> Spark renderer. Dynamic objects: YOLOv8n plus IoU tracker as clusters. Offline path: `tools/sei_to_transforms.py` plus gsplat.

---

## 1. What Tesla actually does (verified Nov 22, 2026)

There are two different Tesla systems, and people online are mixing them up.

### A. Tesla Reality View (FSD v15, started rolling out Nov 21, 2026): a diffusion video generator, not splats
- Tesla's FSD v15 release post lists "Tesla Reality View: Adds a live visualization of the drive that lets you see & control what FSD is planning to do." (https://x.com/Tesla/status/2124222447855743019, Nov 21, 2026)
- Ashok Elluswamy: "The same end-to-end driving model also renders a realistic view of the world around the vehicle." (https://x.com/aelluswamy/status/2124229978560835880)
- Elon Musk: "This is real-time AI video generation (no gaming engine!) using just half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator." (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124270426402836626)
- Elon Musk, the technical detail (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124458557525397780, Nov 22, 2026): the in-car version is "a conditional mean-flow model, based on the recent sequence of Tesla Universal Vision Encoder features, which are the same ones that are plugged into the 3B encoder model that produces the driving actions. We fork off from these UVE features, then run a separate ~1B diffusion model. This takes in the previous reality-view generation with added noise and then denoises it to the current visualization. It then produces a small video chunk (8 images at 36 fps, at the resolution of the UI screen)... 1/2 of a TRIP engine every UVE frame at 18 hz." He adds that a larger "~pixel-perfect world model" has existed "since last year" for synthetic data.
- Follow-up: TRIP is int8 only, and AI5 is "roughly 12X more compute" (https://x.com/elonmusk/status/2124459444620628193).
- So Reality View is pixels generated by a diffusion model conditioned on the driving network's features. There is no explicit 3D gaussian scene. Several Grok auto-replies on X claim Reality View is generative Gaussian splatting. Elon's own description contradicts them.

### B. Tesla Generative Gaussian Splatting: a feed-forward 3D/4D splat head (shown since Oct 2025)
- Ashok Elluswamy's X article "Tesla's approach to Autonomy" (Oct 24, 2025) says traditional 3DGS struggles because "typical vehicle motion... is quite linear", needs initialization, and takes "10s of minutes". Tesla's version "has great generalization, runs in ~220 ms, doesn't require initialization, can model dynamic objects and can be jointly trained with the end-to-end AI model... generated based on the production vehicle configuration's cameras." Source (mirror): https://nitter.gq/aelluswamy/article/1981644831790379245
- The same material appears in his ICCV25 WDFM-AD talk (https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/721812/), his Feb 2026 Matroid talk (https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/721770/), and his CVPR'26 keynote (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2V0ddkF1Svo ; transcript https://ceointerviews.ai/interview/1312221/): "generative Gaussian splatting where we can generate the entire scene as a whole... done in a feedforward manner... purely from just the in-car videos."
- It is used as an interpretability and debug output of the driving network. It is proprietary, trained on fleet data, with no weights or paper.

### C. Ryan Vogel's "recreating Reality View" thread (Nov 21 to 22, 2026)
- https://x.com/ryanvogel/status/2124254845960097888: "a splat of the road that is built from temporal video frames to compare depth"
- https://x.com/ryanvogel/status/2124259810711335049: "ground layer splatting is done... constructed in almost realtime from my dashcam footage"
- https://x.com/ryanvogel/status/2124282699510857746: higher resolution ground, "3D static objects... with accurate lighting and shadows"
- https://x.com/ryanvogel/status/2124464850550292638: "dynamic splats for moving objects like cars and busses"
- He has not published his exact models. His staged structure (ground layer, static objects, dynamic splats) is the same decomposition we already have. Bilawal Sidhu noted that offline 4DGS recreations spend "hours of GPU time per minute of driving" (https://x.com/bilawalsidhu/status/2124672206609240090).

### What this means for us
- We cannot copy Tesla's model. Its weights are closed, and it depends on UVE features trained on fleet data.
- The closest open equivalent to Tesla's generative splatting is the 2025-2026 family of feed-forward driving 4DGS models (DGGT, ReconDrive, PointForward, FreeForward4D; section 2C). These run on CUDA servers and were trained on Waymo or nuScenes rigs.
- A video-diffusion "Reality View look" is out of reach in a browser today. The nearest open option would be a world model or video model on a GPU server.
- The practical wins for a Safari app are better geometry priors: multi-view consistent depth and pose, true metric depth, and temporal consistency. Section 3 ranks them.

## 3. Ranked recommendation

### Pick 1: best in-browser upgrade to ship now. Depth Anything 3 replaces DA-V2-Small, with odometry-locked scale
Why: Apache-2.0, ONNX already on HF, runs on Safari 26 WebGPU (Safari 26 ships WebGPU on macOS Tahoe/iOS 26: https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/). DA3's multi-view mode gives depth that agrees across frames, which is our biggest visual artifact source today (ghost layers and wobble that the consensus refine fights).

Integration plan (in `src/reconstruct/depth.ts`, `metricDepth.ts`, `capabilities.ts`):
1. Tier A (default on M-series with WebGPU): `Heliosoph/da3-base-4view-onnx` `model_fp16.onnx` (207 MB) through onnxruntime-web/webgpu. Run per camera on sliding 4-frame windows (stride ~3 frames, 1-frame overlap between windows). Inputs 504x504, ImageNet normalization.
2. Scale: for each window, s = |telemetry displacement between view 0 and view 3| / |predicted translation|. Cross-check against the camera-height ground fit already in `metricDepth.ts`. Use the median of both when they agree within 15%, and fall back to camera height when the car is stopped (displacement < 0.5 m).
3. Use the predicted relative rotations only as a sanity check against the rig and telemetry pose (flag windows that disagree > 3 deg). Keep telemetry as the pose source.
4. Use `depth_conf` to drop low-confidence pixels before back-projection (replaces part of the consensus refine).
5. Tier B (low memory, or no WebGPU, WASM): `onnx-community/depth-anything-v3-small` single-view (105 MB, 5D input with num_images=1). Note: depth, not disparity, so invert the old normalization.
6. Optional in the same round: `onnx-community/metric3d-vit-small` fp16 (76 MB) as a metric anchor per keyframe using HW3 focal lengths, if odometry scale is unreliable.
7. Cache models in Cache Storage. Keep DA-V2-Small as an emergency fallback for one release.

Pass/fail checks:
- Loads and runs in Safari 26 on Tone's Mac with WebGPU, with no tab crash on a 1-minute 4-camera event. Peak JS + GPU memory under ~3 GB (Safari Web Inspector timeline).
- Build time for a 1-minute event at most 2x the current Round 3 time.
- Consistency: median reprojection depth error between overlapping consecutive frames (warp frame t into t+1 using telemetry pose) improves by at least 25% vs DA-V2-Small on the same clip.
- Scale: odometry scale and camera-height scale agree within 15% on at least 80% of moving windows.
- Visual: ground plane flatness (RMS of road-labelled points from a fitted plane) improves. No double curbs or ghost cars in an orbit 10 m off the driven path.
- Existing 25 tests still pass. Add a unit test for the 5D input shape and the depth (not disparity) convention.

### Pick 2: best quality path with a Mac-local step. MapAnything (MLX) metric geometry, then Mac splat training with static/dynamic split
Why: MapAnything's Apache variant runs natively on Apple Silicon via mlx-vlm. It accepts known intrinsics and poses as inputs, so we can give it our HW3 calibration plus telemetry poses and get metric, multi-view consistent pointmaps for all 4 cameras in one model. That is much better initialization than per-frame mono depth. Then train real gaussians on the Mac with mpsplat or OpenSplat (MPS) instead of CUDA gsplat.

Integration plan (new `tools/mac/` scripts; browser stays the viewer):
1. Reuse `tools/sei_to_transforms.py` to emit frames plus per-frame K and camera-to-world poses (ENU) for all 4 cameras.
2. `tools/mac/geom_mapanything.py`: `mlx_vlm.load("mlx-community/map-anything-apache-fp16")`. Run on chunks of about 16-32 views (mixed cameras and times, with overlap), passing K and poses. Output metric pts3d plus conf. Fuse to a point cloud, masking YOLO/tracker dynamic boxes (reuse the existing tracker logic in Python via ultralytics, or export the masks from the browser).
3. Optional depth upgrade for training supervision: DA3-Large-1.1 or Depth Pro 2 CoreML (research-only license) per frame as a depth prior.
4. `tools/mac/train_mps.sh`: static background with mpsplat (gsplat-API compatible) or OpenSplat MPS, initialized from step 2, with depth loss and per-camera exposure/affine color (MCSplat idea) to fix HW3 repeater vs front color mismatch. Dynamic objects: per-track rigid gaussian sets (OmniRe-style) driven by tracker boxes, exported as separate clusters in our existing dynamic format.
5. Export `.ply` / `.splat` into the viewer's existing loader (`src/export/*`, Spark).

Pass/fail checks:
- Runs end-to-end on Tone's M-series Mac with no CUDA. A 1-minute 4-camera event finishes in at most 60 min. MapAnything stage fits in unified memory (no swap storm).
- Held-out-frame PSNR (every 8th frame withheld) at least 2 dB above the browser Pick 1 result on the same event. LPIPS improves.
- Novel view 3-5 m lateral offset shows no floaters across the road surface, judged on a fixed set of 5 viewpoints.
- Metric check: lane width measured in the splat between 3.0 and 3.9 m on a US road. Car length of a parked car between 4 and 5.5 m.
- Moving cars render as coherent objects (no smears) in at least 90% of tracked frames.
- License note: MapAnything-apache and mpsplat/OpenSplat are fine to share. Depth Pro 2, VGGT-Omega, DA3 Giant are research-only, so keep them optional and never bundle them into the public site.

### Pick 3: closest to Tesla's generative splatting. DGGT feed-forward 4D on a rented NVIDIA GPU
Why: public checkpoints, pose-free, dynamic head, predicts gaussians directly in one pass, and reported zero-shot transfer across Waymo/nuScenes/Argoverse2. This is the open model most like Ashok's description. It needs CUDA (cloud GPU or a friend's box), not Safari or MLX.

Plan: run `xiaomi-research/dggt` with `model_latest_waymo.pth` on 4-camera HW3 frame windows. Convert its per-frame gaussians to our dynamic splat format. Compare against Pick 2 on the same event.
Pass/fail: runs on one 24 GB+ GPU. Per-window inference under 5 s. Held-out PSNR within 1 dB of Pick 2 at under 10% of the time, or it is dropped. License: VGGT-derived parts carry VGGT terms (CC BY-NC for the original checkpoint), so keep it personal and non-commercial.

### Watch list
- FreeForward4D (RGB-only feed-forward 4D, code "will be released"): the best conceptual match for dashcams once code ships.
- VGGT-Omega (dynamic plus long sequence, MLX available, non-commercial): a drop-in alternative to MapAnything for Pick 2 if MapAnything struggles with forward-only motion.
- A Reality View-style diffusion "look" would need an open driving world model on a GPU server. Not feasible in Safari today.

### Not recommended for the browser
VGGT/VGGT-Omega/Pi3/MapAnything/AnySplat in the browser (2-5 GB weights; Safari tabs will not hold them). MASt3R/MonST3R (non-commercial, optimization-heavy, superseded). Driving feed-forward models trained on 6-camera rigs (DrivingForward, Omni-Scene, EVolSplat) as primary paths (CUDA, rig mismatch).
